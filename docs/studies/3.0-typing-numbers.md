# Where typing stands in 3.0, in every language

Measured on main at `4d6959c4` on 2026-11-17: corrections in every language, next words, and joining a swipe to the touch after it. The numbers come from the repo's own suites and the #83 replay, so anyone can run them again ("How to run it again", at the end).

## In short

- **English** fixes about 7 in 10 slips in whole chat sentences, and changes almost no correct word: 0 of 1,052 in the chat messages, 6 of 18,381 in 3,000 held-out sentences.
- **44 other languages** with a word list now fix slips on the keys:
  - Space puts in the right word for 56.4% of slips, and a wrong one for 0.1%. A correct word is never changed.
  - The right word is on the bar for 91.3%.
  - Greek, Galician and Slovak show their fixes on the bar only, since Space would have been wrong too often there.
- **Hindi and Vietnamese** offer the word one slipped key away, on the bar only: the right word is there for 76% and 80% of slips, up from 6% and 16%.
- **Next words** after a word exist in English, Chinese and Japanese only. The other languages show their starters at a sentence's start, but nothing after a word. That's the biggest gap left.
- **Joins:** 377 of 380 right joins are kept. 54 of 64 wrong joins still happen; the rule cuts 10.

## Corrections

**Other languages: the test.** For each language, up to 300 of its commonest words with one made-up slip each:
- a neighbouring key on its own layout;
- a letter left out;
- two letters swapped;
- the accents left off ("cafe" for café).

A slip that is itself a word is left out, since no single-word check can tell it. **Space right** and **Space wrong** are what Space puts in; **on the bar** is the right word among the bar's words. `tests/corrections`.

**English is measured differently.** Its suite types whole sentences with 12% of their words slipped, real-word slips included, so its numbers are harder and not directly comparable. `tests/autocorrect`.

**Shipped tables vs the study:** the shipped Chinese tables are built from all of Tatoeba, with Mandarin converted to Traditional first, so they're larger than the study's. Measured again on text they don't hold, Common Voice's sentences (zh-HK 19,020, zh-TW 15,580; `native/rime/predict_fallback.py`, 2026-11-17), the next character is among the first 3 after 8.3% of words in Cantonese and 4.3% in Mandarin: Common Voice is further from Tatoeba's everyday sentences than the study's held-out Tatoeba was.

**After a phrase committed at once** (item 5): librime-predict looked up the whole commit, so a phrase of 2 to 4 words got no suggestions at all (0%). TitanKeys now tries what it ends with (`native/rime/tk_predict.h`): suggestions show after 94.0% of them in Cantonese and 95.2% in Mandarin, and the next character is among the first 3 for 7.2% and 4.1%, about as for words committed one at a time.

**No tables yet:** no next-word tables (`pairs.txt`) for the other languages are on main or any branch yet.
