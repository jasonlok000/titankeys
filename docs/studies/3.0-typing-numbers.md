# Where typing stands in 3.0, in every language

Measured on main at `2dc82dbb` on 2026-11-17. It covers corrections in every language, both on single words and mid-sentence with word pairs; next words; and joining a swipe to the touch after it. The numbers come from the repo's own suites and the #83 replay, so anyone can run them again ("How to run it again", at the end).

## In short

- **English** fixes about 7 in 10 slips in whole chat sentences and changes almost no correct word: 0 of 1,052 in the chat messages, 6 of 18,381 in 3,000 held-out sentences.
- **44 other languages** with a word list fix slips on the keys:
  - **Mid-sentence,** with the word before helping: Space puts in the right word for **72.0%** of slips and a wrong one for **0.5%**. Without the word pairs it was 68.6%.
  - **Single words,** with no sentence around them: Space is right for **60.2%** and wrong for **0.1%**. The right word is on the bar for **92.3%**. A correct word is never changed.
  - **Serbian (Cyrillic)** shows its fixes on the bar only: Space would be wrong for 1.2% of its slips mid-sentence, and the limit is 1.0%.
  - **Georgian** corrects without word pairs: they took its wrong changes from 0.7% to 1.0%.
- **Hindi and Vietnamese** offer the word one slipped key away, on the bar only. The right word is there for 76% and 80% of slips, up from 6% and 16%.
- **Next words:** every input method with a word list now has a next-word table, 48 files from the Leipzig corpora. Inside a sentence, the right next word is among the bar's first three about a fifth to a third of the time.
- **Joins:** 377 of 380 right joins are kept. 54 of 64 wrong joins still happen; the rule cuts 10.

## Corrections

### Mid-sentence, with word pairs

**The test.** For each language, 600 words from the first 20,000 sentences of its Leipzig corpus, each given one made-up slip (the kinds below), with the sentence's earlier words typed before it. Each language's next-word table (its `pairs.bin`) weighs the candidates by the word before, as English's pairs do. The table keeps each word's 8 commonest followers in order but not their counts, so a follower counts by its place: 256 for the first, halving down the list. `tests/corrections` with `CORRECTIONS_SENTENCES` set to the Leipzig folder.

**The rules it writes:**
- `corrections-bar-only.txt`: a language whose Space is wrong for 1.0% or more of its slips here shows its fixes on the bar only.
- `corrections-no-pairs.txt`: a language whose pairs add more than 1 wrong change in 600 words corrects without them.

The tables come from the same corpora as the test sentences, so the gain from pairs is an upper bound.

### Single words

**The test.** For each language, up to 300 of its commonest words with one made-up slip each:
- a neighbouring key on its own layout;
- a letter left out;
- two letters swapped;
- the accents left off ("cafe" for café).

A slip that is itself a word is left out, since no single-word check can tell it. **Space right** and **Space wrong** are what Space puts in; **on the bar** is the right word among the bar's words. There's no word before, so pairs don't help here. `tests/corrections`.

**English is measured differently.** Its suite types whole sentences with 12% of their words slipped, real-word slips included, so its numbers are harder and not directly comparable. `tests/autocorrect`.

## Next words

Every input method with a word list now has a next-word table: `android/assets/im/<id>/pairs.bin`, 48 files (Portuguese has one for Brazil and one for Portugal), built from the Leipzig corpora. Each holds the 8 commonest next words per word, and `<s>` for a sentence's start. The bar shows them after a word, and corrections use them as the word before. [next-words.md](../next-words.md) has how they're made, their size and their memory.

| Input method | After a word | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| English | the swipe engine's next-word model and learned pairs | not measured in this pass |
| Cantonese (Yale, Jyutping) | librime-predict, word pairs from Tatoeba | right next character in the first 3: 30.9% on held-out sentences ([56-chinese-prediction.md](56-chinese-prediction.md)) |
| Mandarin (Pinyin) | librime-predict, word pairs from Tatoeba | 18.9% |
| Japanese | Mozc's own suggestions after a pick | not measured |
| The 44 other languages, Hindi, Vietnamese, Korean, Thai | the language's `pairs.bin`, from Leipzig | right next word in the first 3, inside a sentence, on held-out sentences: Hindi 35.6%, Vietnamese 31.2%, Spanish 28.6%, Thai 27.4%, Russian 23.3%, Korean 22.3%, Belarusian 22.0%, German 20.1%, Georgian 20.1% |

Languages that pack grammar into word endings (Korean, Georgian, Russian, German) score lower: there are more words to choose from. A held-out sentence's first word has a table 97.3% to 100% of the time.

**Shipped Chinese tables vs the study:** the shipped Chinese tables are built from all of Tatoeba, with Mandarin converted to Traditional first, so they're larger than the study's. Measured again on text they don't hold, Common Voice's sentences (zh-HK 19,020, zh-TW 15,580; `native/rime/predict_fallback.py`, 2026-11-17), the next character is among the first 3 after 8.3% of words in Cantonese and 4.3% in Mandarin: Common Voice is further from Tatoeba's everyday sentences than the study's held-out Tatoeba was.

**After a phrase committed at once** (item 5): librime-predict looked up the whole commit, so a phrase of 2 to 4 words got no suggestions at all (0%). TitanKeys now tries what it ends with (`native/rime/tk_predict.h`): suggestions show after 94.0% of them in Cantonese and 95.2% in Mandarin, and the next character is among the first 3 for 7.2% and 4.1%, about as for words committed one at a time.
